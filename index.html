About this map

This map supports the SEASTRONG desk-based screening of candidate mangrove, seagrass and coral restoration sites in southern Belize, prepared for the pre-visit with TIDE and Healthy Reefs for Healthy People (24 November to 3 December 2026). The screening is preliminary: site locations, scores and water clarity estimates are intended to guide field visits and need checking with partners and in the field.

Prepared by Graham Patterson (REEF Scuba) for the SEASTRONG project (Horizon Europe, grant agreement No. 101286483).

Candidate sites

Clarity-screened sites R1 to R13 each combine a mangrove, a seagrass and a coral point. They are the current shortlist for field assessment.

First-pass corridors C1 to C11 are mangrove, seagrass and coral transects chosen to sample a protection gradient (no-take, general use, unprotected) and a connectivity gradient (coral distance to the nearest mangrove: tight, less than 1 km; intermediate, 1 to 5 km; loose, 5 to 15 km). Five of them (C1, C2, C5, C6 and C7) do not pass the water clarity screen described below.

Mangrove plot candidates M1 to M12 are patches mapped as mangrove in 2015 but not in 2025 (Global Mangrove Watch v4.1). The assessment separates apparent seaward-fringe loss (priority) from probable changes in classification at the landward ecotone (to be verified).

Seagrass plot candidates S1 to S10 are bare patches inside continuous seagrass (Allen Coral Atlas), ranked by distance from the Abalone Caye ranger station.

How sites R1 to R13 were selected

Candidates. Coral points were placed on a 200 m grid inside Allen Coral Atlas coral/algae polygons. Each was paired with the nearest mangrove (Global Mangrove Watch v3 2020, supplemented by first-pass v4.1 points where v3 shows no mangrove) and a seagrass point from a 100 m seagrass grid between them. The three points of a site are at least 250 m apart, the mangrove lies within 5 km of the coral point, and no point lies within 1.5 km of another site.

Clarity screen. Sites were retained only where the year-round median turbidity was at most 2 FNU at the coral point and at most 2.5 FNU at the seagrass point.

Suitability score (0 to 1). Water clarity 40 percent (median turbidity and the share of cloud-free days above 5 FNU, averaged over the coral and seagrass points); habitat extent 20 percent (coral/algae within 400 m, seagrass within 300 m, mangrove within 500 m); connectivity 15 percent (coral to mangrove distance); access from Punta Gorda 15 percent (full marks within 15 km, none beyond 60 km); distance from river mouths 10 percent. The weights reflect the priority given to water clarity for repeated scuba-based work; they are judgments rather than calibrated values.

Selection. The highest-scoring sites were taken within each protection class, at least 2.5 km apart, to retain the contrast between no-take, general use and unprotected areas: three Port Honduras Marine Reserve no-take sites (R2, R4, R6), four general use sites (R1, R3, R5, R7) and two unprotected sites (R8, R9) within 50 km of Punta Gorda, two sites in the Sapodilla Cayes (R10, R11) and two unprotected sites in the northern lagoon (R12, R13), which are more accessible from Placencia. Clear water in central Port Honduras lies almost entirely inside the reserve, so the clear-water unprotected sites are in the northern lagoon; those near Punta Gorda are moderately clear.

Not yet assessed: depth at each point, substrate firmness, currents, land tenure of caye mangroves and landing access.

Water clarity

Turbidity was estimated from 1,200 Sentinel-2 Level-2A scenes (2020 to 2025; 447 usable acquisition dates) using the single-band algorithm of Dogliotti et al. (2015), with the red band switching to near-infrared in turbid water. Sun glint and residual haze were removed by subtracting short-wave infrared reflectance; observations with strong glint or haze were discarded, as were clouds and cloud shadows (buffered by 240 m). A per-date correction scaled the clearest offshore water to about 0.2 FNU. Results are gridded at 120 m.

Values are best read as relative guides: below 1 FNU very clear, 1 to 2 clear, 2 to 5 moderate, 5 to 10 turbid, above 10 plume water. They are not estimates of underwater visibility in meters. Secchi disk readings taken during the pre-visit would allow the layer to be calibrated.

  • Clear-sky bias. Optical satellites observe only cloud-free days. The wet season has 25 to 34 usable dates per month against 40 to 58 in the dry season, so plumes after heavy rain are under-sampled. Wet-season values are best treated as a lower bound.
  • Bright bottom. In clear water shallower than about 3 m, light reflected from sand can raise the signal. Shallow reef flats on the barrier reef and in the Sapodilla Cayes are masked in gray. Smaller shallow areas are not, and the seagrass plot candidates (bare sand) may read high.
  • Seasonal pattern. Many inshore sites read higher in the dry season than in the wet season. This may partly reflect resuspension by trade winds and winter cold fronts in shallow Port Honduras, and partly residual haze from the burning season.
  • The signal comes mainly from the upper 1 to 3 m of the water column in turbid water. Points in narrow creeks or against mangrove fringes are sampled from the nearest open water; M1 and M8 have no water pixels within 1.2 km.
  • A small step between Sentinel-2 tiles is visible near 16.3°N.

Depth and reef monitoring

Bathymetry comes from the NOAA NCEI DEM Global Mosaic. In this area its cells are about 460 m, consistent with the GEBCO 15 arc-second grid. It shows the lagoon floor (half of the lagoon cells are 17 to 36 m deep), the Snake Cayes and Sapodilla shoals and the drop-off east of the barrier reef. It does not resolve reef tops, patch reefs or channels between cayes, so depths at coral points can read several meters too deep.

AGRRA data come from the public services behind the AGRRA Data Explorer. Benthic surveys give live coral and fleshy macroalgae cover and the Reef Health Index (site-level public results run 2011 to 2021); 17 of them were fish-only, and their index reflects fish indicators alone. Coral health surveys (2017 to 2026) count colonies by condition; recent Placencia-area records are largely Fragments of Hope monitoring. Records marked as not approved are excluded, and surveys with fewer than 10 colonies are treated as qualitative. The map loads the latest records when it opens and falls back to a copy saved on 13 October 2026.The map loads the records live from AGRRA each time it opens; no AGRRA data are stored in this site.

Live imagery

Daily imagery from NASA GIBS: MODIS Terra and Aqua and VIIRS true color (250 to 375 m), PACE OCI true color and chlorophyll-a (about 1.2 km) and Harmonized Landsat and Sentinel-2 (30 m, on overpass days only). PACE resolves lagoon-scale and Gulf of Honduras plumes but not individual inshore cayes.

Other limitations

  • The mangrove plot candidates come from Global Mangrove Watch v4.1 (2015 to 2025); the mangrove layer on this map is v3 (2010 to 2020), so the two do not align exactly. The Figures tab shows the original v4.1 maps.
  • The reef connectivity coloring is computed from the v3 2020 mangrove extent and may differ slightly from the first-pass figures.
  • The Port Honduras no-take zones are the four small reserve parts in the WDPA, matching the replenishment zones used in the first pass. Current zoning should be confirmed with the Fisheries Department and TIDE; Sapodilla Cayes zoning is not attributed in the WDPA.
  • Allen Coral Atlas mapping is generally considered reliable to about 10 m depth in clear water; turbid inshore areas are likely under-mapped.
  • Distances are straight-line, not boat routes. Land tenure at M3 and M6 is unknown.
