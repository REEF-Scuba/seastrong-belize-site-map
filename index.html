What this map shows

Corridors C1–C11 are mangrove → seagrass → reef transects chosen to sample connected habitat across a protection gradient (no-take, general use, unprotected) and a connectivity gradient (reef distance to nearest mangrove: tight <1 km, intermediate 1–5 km, loose 5–15 km).

Mangrove plot candidates M1–M12 are patches mapped as mangrove in 2015 but not in 2025 (Global Mangrove Watch v4.1). The assessment column flags which look like real seaward-fringe loss (priority) versus probable classification shifts at the landward ecotone (verify on the ground).

Seagrass plot candidates S1–S10 are bare patches inside continuous seagrass (Allen Coral Atlas), ranked by distance from Abalone Caye ranger station. sg_frac_ring is the fraction of the surrounding ring classed as seagrass, a proxy for how enclosed the gap is.

Clarity-screened sites R1–R13: how they were picked

Candidates. Coral nodes on a 200 m grid inside every Allen Coral Atlas Coral/Algae polygon (2,644 points). For each one: the nearest mangrove (GMW v3 2020, plus the v4.1 scouting points where v3 has no mangrove, e.g. the Sapodilla and outer cayes), and a seagrass node picked from a 100 m seagrass grid between them. The seagrass pick trades off staying on the reef–mangrove path against the clearest available water. Within a site, the three nodes are at least 250 m apart so each shows separately when zoomed in, and the mangrove is within 5 km of the coral node so each site works as one compact area. No node is within 1.5 km of another site’s nodes.

Screen. Coral-node median turbidity ≤ 2 FNU and seagrass-node median ≤ 2.5 FNU (all-year, Sentinel-2 2020–25).

Score (0–1). Water clarity 40% (median turbidity and the share of clear-sky days above 5 FNU, averaged over the coral and seagrass nodes) · habitat extent 20% (coral within 400 m, seagrass within 300 m, mangrove within 500 m) · connectivity 15% (tight <1 km scores highest) · access from Punta Gorda 15% (≤15 km best, ≥60 km zero) · distance from river mouths 10%.

Selection. Highest-scoring sites, filled by protection class so the set keeps the no-take / general-use / unprotected contrast. Within 50 km of Punta Gorda: 3 PHMR no-take (R2, R4, R6), 4 PHMR general use (R1, R3, R5, R7) and 2 unprotected (R8, R9). Further out: 2 at the Sapodilla Cayes (R10, R11) and 2 unprotected in the northern lagoon (R12, R13, better run from Placencia). The clear central PHMR water is almost all inside the reserve. Unprotected controls there would share mangrove stands with the no-take sites, so the clear-water unprotected sites are in the northern lagoon, and the ones near Punta Gorda are only moderately clear.

Effect on the first pass. Five original corridors fail the clarity screen: C1 (coral 2.1 / seagrass 2.4 FNU), C2 (2.3 / 2.5), C5 (3.0 / 3.5), C6 (3.6 / 4.4) and C7 (2.8 / 2.9). C3, C4, C8, C9, C10 and C11 pass. C3 and C4 are in the same Snake Cayes area as R2 and R4. Turbid inshore reefs aren’t worthless scientifically (their corals are adapted to sediment and may be useful as a contrast or refuge), but they’re poor places for repeated diving work.

Not yet known from desk data: depth at each node, substrate firmness, current, tenure of caye mangroves, and boat landing access. Check these on the pre-visit.

Water clarity layer: how it was made

Turbidity (FNU) from 1,200 Sentinel-2 L2A scenes, Jan 2020–Dec 2025 (447 usable acquisition dates), Microsoft Planetary Computer. Per scene: Dogliotti et al. (2015) single-band algorithm, red 665 nm switching to NIR 865 nm in turbid water; sun glint and aerosol residual removed by subtracting SWIR (2190 nm) reflectance; observations with SWIR > 0.02 discarded as glint/haze (~48% of clear-sky pixel observations); clouds, shadows and cirrus masked with the scene classification and buffered 240 m; then a per-date dark-water correction scaling the clearest 10% of offshore water to ~0.2 FNU (removes Mar–May smoke haze). Gridded at 120 m. Seasonal layers show the median of all clear-sky observations and the share of them above 5 FNU.

Reading the classes for diving: <1 FNU very clear; 1–2 clear; 2–5 moderate, reduced visibility; 5–10 turbid, poor visibility; >10 plume water. These are relative guides, not visibility in metres. Take a Secchi disk on the pre-visit and log readings at each site; a few in-situ values would let us calibrate this layer to visibility.

Caveats specific to water clarity

  • Clear-sky bias. Optical satellites only see cloud-free days. In the wet season (25–34 usable dates per month vs 40–58 in the dry season) the days right after heavy rain, when plumes peak, are under-sampled. Treat wet-season values as a best case.
  • Bright bottom. In clear water shallower than ~3 m, light reflected off sand raises the signal and reads as “turbid”. Shallow reef flats and sand on the barrier reef and Sapodilla Cayes are flagged grey and excluded. Smaller inshore shallows aren’t flagged: the seagrass gap sites (S1–S10) are bare sand, so their values may be inflated.
  • Dry season reads higher than wet at many inshore sites. Part of this is probably real: Feb–May trade winds and winter cold fronts (“northers”) stir up sediment in shallow Port Honduras. Part may be leftover haze from the burning season. Check it against the monthly profiles and field observations.
  • Surface-weighted: the satellite sees roughly the top 1–3 m in turbid water. A clearer bottom layer (or a turbid near-bottom layer) can be missed.
  • At 120 m, sites inside narrow creeks or tight against mangrove fringes are sampled from the nearest open water (shown in each popup). M1 and M8 (Sarstoon-Temash) have no reliable water pixels.
  • A small step between Sentinel-2 tiles is visible around 16.3°N.

Depth and reef monitoring

Bathymetry comes from NOAA NCEI’s global DEM mosaic. Over southern Belize its cells are about 460 m, consistent with the GEBCO 15-arc-second grid. It shows the lagoon floor (half the lagoon cells are 17–36 m), the Snake Cayes and Sapodilla shoals, and the drop-off east of the barrier reef. It does not resolve reef tops, patch reefs or channels between cayes: a coral node on a 2 m reef flat can read 15–20 m. Treat popup depths as context only.

AGRRA data come from the public layers behind the AGRRA Data Explorer. Benthic surveys (BaseBySite) give live coral and fleshy macroalgae cover and the Reef Health Index; public site-level results run 2011–2021. Coral health surveys (Coral Health Survey, 2017–2026) count colonies by condition: healthy, SCTLD, other disease, fully or partially bleached, pale, recently dead. Recent Placencia-area data are largely Fragments of Hope’s monthly bleaching surveys. The map loads them live from AGRRA each time it opens; no AGRRA data are stored in this site. Unapproved coral-health records are left out, and surveys with fewer than 10 colonies counted as qualitative. The data cover the map’s window (15.85–16.6°N, 89.25–87.95°W), so a few Guatemalan sites in the Bay of Amatique are included.

Live imagery

Pulled daily from NASA GIBS: MODIS Terra/Aqua and VIIRS true colour (250–375 m), PACE OCI true colour and chlorophyll-a (~1.2 km, daily), and Harmonized Landsat–Sentinel-2 (30 m, only on overpass days, often cloudy). PACE resolves lagoon-scale and Gulf of Honduras plumes (Sarstún, Motagua, Dulce) but not individual inshore cayes. PACE’s quantitative diffuse-attenuation product, Kd(490), isn’t on GIBS. It needs a free NASA Earthdata login to download and could be added as a climatology later.

Caveats

  • Mangrove version mismatch. The M1–M12 candidates were derived from GMW v4.1 (2015→2025). The mangrove layer here is GMW v3 (2010→2020) because v4.1 isn’t available as a web service. Expect the M polygons not to line up exactly with the v3 loss pixels; the Figures tab shows the original v4.1 maps.
  • Reef connectivity colouring is recomputed here from GMW v3 2020 extent (distance from each Coral/Algae polygon to nearest mangrove), so class boundaries may differ slightly from the scouting figure.
  • PHMR no-take zones are the four small Port Honduras Marine Reserve sub-polygons in WDPA, which match the replenishment zones used in the scouting analysis. Confirm current zoning with TIDE and the Fisheries Department before fieldwork. Sapodilla Cayes MR zoning is not attributed in WDPA.
  • Allen Coral Atlas is a remote-sensing product, validated to roughly 10 m depth in clear water; turbid inshore Port Honduras waters are likely under-mapped.
  • Tenure for M3 and M6 is unknown; check before approaching landowners.
